Three unusual things in the per-sample summary

1. Near-monocultures are the rule, not the exception, in the species table:
   50 of the 471 species samples have a single species holding more than 99%
   of the reads (e.g. P011_week_18: one species at 99.7%, only 4 species
   detected). In a pregnancy vaginal microbiome this is biology - usually one
   dominant Lactobacillus - not a mistake.

2. The gene table's problem children are single-gene dominators: one gene in
   P150_week_34 holds 32% of that sample (typical is 8%), dragging its Shannon
   to 5.0 against a median of 6.95. Worth checking whether that gene is real
   biology or an annotation/alignment artefact.

3. Depth and diversity track each other in the genes: even the worst gene
   sample saw 96% of all 14,226 genes, the opposite of the species table where
   samples saw as few as 4 of 220 species.

Checks:
- filter(share_largest > 0.99) returns 50 rows, all species
- filter(sample == "P150_week_34"): share_largest 0.324, shannon 5.00
- min(features_detected[table == "genes"]) / 14226 = 0.963
